Podcast SEO Clinic Series: Quick Wins & Troubleshooting Guide ​

Welcome to the Podcast SEO Clinic—your go-to resource for practical checks for common distribution and discovery problems. Use the findings to choose a change, then check whether the relevant outcome improves.

Choose the right first check ​

  • A new episode is missing: check the feed for an error, then check the listing in the affected app. Fix a distribution issue before changing keywords.
  • The episode is present but hard to understand at a glance: make its title accurately describe the subject and benefit. For example, “Episode 12” could become “Growing Tomatoes in Pots: A Beginner’s Balcony Guide.”
  • A tracked position changed: compare the same term, country and platform, and note recent releases or promotions. One movement alone does not reveal the cause.

Use Keyword Tracker to record a starting point and review the same search later; a position is not a listener count.

The 15-Minute SEO Audit: Your Podcast's Health Check ​

Set aside about 15 minutes for an initial review; fixing a problem may take longer. Use this checkup to identify issues rather than as a promise of growth. Here's your systematic approach to auditing your show's search performance.

A discovery checkup

Identify an issue before choosing a change.

  1. Check distributionReview the RSS feed and listings. Look for errors or missing episodes.
  2. Review your contentDo recent episode titles name the topic? Is the show description useful and clear?
  3. Choose and reviewRecord a change; compare its relevant outcome in the available reports.

An initial check takes about 15 minutes; fixing an issue can take longer.

Start with your RSS feed—it's essentially your podcast's DNA. Open your feed URL in a browser and look for these critical elements: proper category tags, episode-level descriptions, and consistent metadata structure. Malformed RSS can prevent directories from updating a show or accepting an episode; it does not tell you how many listeners were lost.

For a comprehensive analysis of your RSS feed, PodSEO's Distribution Health can automatically identify these technical issues and provide specific recommendations for improvement.

Your episode titles deserve special attention during this audit. Pull up your last ten episodes and ask yourself: would someone searching for solutions in your niche actually type these words? If your titles read like creative writing exercises rather than search queries, you've found your first optimization opportunity.

Check the actual feed URL and the listing in each podcast app. A Google search for your show plus ‘RSS’ is not a feed-validation test: a healthy feed need not appear near the top of web results. Use a validator for feed errors and website indexing tools for your episode pages.

Common Optimization Issues to Check ​

The most expensive SEO mistakes aren't always the most obvious ones. Start with issues you can verify in your feed, listings and reports. Let's tackle the big three that trip up even experienced creators.

Mistake #1: Category Confusion Your category helps place the show in relevant browsing and chart contexts. Choose an accurate category; changing it does not guarantee better keyword search positions. The key insight? Choose categories based on where your ideal listeners are browsing, not where you think you logically belong.

Mistake #2: Description Neglect Most creators write their show descriptions once and forget them. Descriptions help listeners decide whether the show fits their interests. Spotify recommends specific, searchable descriptions; Apple's published search inputs do not list descriptions. Your description should read like a compelling elevator pitch that happens to include your target keywords naturally.

Mistake #3: Inconsistent Publishing Patterns A sustainable schedule helps listeners know when to expect an episode. There is no established universal ranking bonus for a particular weekday or time. More episodes can increase total downloads without increasing the number of listeners.

Experiments When Your Numbers Level Off ​

Sometimes your podcast growth stalls despite doing everything "right." Use the following ideas as experiments after checking that the apparent stall is not a reporting gap or a change in release frequency.

The Cross-Pollination Strategy This technique involves creating content bridges between your existing episodes. Go back to your most popular episodes and create follow-up content that references them directly. For example, if "Email Marketing Basics" performed well, create "Email Marketing Basics: Six Months Later" or "Advanced Email Marketing for [Your Niche]."

For more strategic approaches to episode titles that drive discovery, see our guide on Episode Title Formulas That Actually Work.

Related follow-up episodes and useful links give listeners a path through your archive. Whether they also increase search discovery needs to be measured.

The Keyword Expansion Play Most creators optimize for their primary keywords but ignore long-tail opportunities. If you're ranking for "productivity tips," you might be missing "productivity tips for remote workers" or "productivity tips for new managers." These longer phrases often have less competition and more qualified searchers.

Use your existing high-performing content as a foundation, then create variations that target these specific sub-niches. Record the terms and episodes you change, then compare their search positions and available traffic reports. A longer phrase can also have very little demand; specificity alone does not guarantee traffic or a deadline for improvement.

To systematically implement this strategy, consider using PodSEO's Keyword Strategy feature to identify your keywords and say what each one is for: your show, an episode, your brand, a competitor, or a theme.

Platform Policy Updates: Staying Ahead of the Curve ​

Platform guidance can change. Check the source and date before changing a working process; a change in your metrics does not by itself prove an algorithm update.

Apple lists metadata, popularity and engagement from search results among its search factors. That is not an announcement of a new completion-rate weighting or an ideal episode length. Check Apple's search guidance before treating an alleged algorithm change as fact.

Spotify's SEO guidance recommends clear titles and descriptions, accurate transcripts and useful episode information. It does not establish a cross-platform engagement formula or a best posting hour.

The Technical Translation When platforms announce algorithm updates, they rarely explain the practical implications for creators. Use this framework for translating these changes into actionable strategies:

  1. Check the official announcement and distinguish a documented requirement from a ranking hypothesis
  2. Analyze your current performance against that metric
  3. Implement targeted improvements (like tighter episode structure)
  4. Monitor results and adjust accordingly

Small Improvements to Check and Review ​

The most effective SEO improvements aren't always the flashiest ones. Sometimes the smallest changes create the biggest long-term impact. Here are the quick wins that experienced creators swear by.

The Timestamp Optimization Timestamps help listeners navigate to a useful passage. Adding ten timestamps does not create ten independently indexed pages or guarantee additional search results. Use clear labels, and publish a useful episode page where web readers can find the notes.

PodSEO's Planner can help you optimize your episode descriptions and show notes for maximum search visibility.

The Strategic Keyword Placement Lead with the episode's specific subject and reason to listen, because apps may truncate the preview. Spotify recommends making the first 250 characters useful; this is not a universal ranking weight or a rule for every platform. Make the opening useful before someone expands the rest.

The Cross-Episode Link Strategy Create connections between your episodes through strategic mentions and references. On your website, link to the related episode page with descriptive text. A spoken reference alone is not a crawlable web link. This technique is particularly powerful for evergreen content that stays relevant over time.

The Troubleshooting Mindset ​

Successful podcast SEO isn't about finding the perfect formula—it's about developing diagnostic skills that help you adapt to changing conditions. The creators who thrive are those who can quickly identify what's working, what's not, and what needs adjustment.

Think of your podcast as a living system rather than a static entity. Your SEO strategy should evolve with your content, audience, and the broader podcast ecosystem. The goal isn't to achieve perfect optimization once, but to maintain consistently strong discoverability as the landscape changes.

Regular audits, strategic experimentation, and data-driven adjustments will serve you better than any single optimization technique. Because at the end of the day, the best SEO strategy is creating valuable content that people actually want to find—and then making sure they can find it.
