Podcast Analytics Decoded: Which Metrics Actually Matter for SEO ​

Download numbers dominate podcast conversations, but on their own they tell you very little about whether people can find your show. A podcast with 50,000 downloads per episode can be almost invisible in search, while a show with 5,000 downloads can be steadily climbing for the keywords that matter to it.

The difference is which metrics you look at, and — just as important — which tool you look at them in. This guide explains what Apple, Spotify, and PodSEO each report, what the platforms say they actually measure, and how to build a review routine that compares your show against its own baseline rather than against numbers nobody can verify.

What the Platforms Say They Measure ​

Before choosing metrics, it helps to know what the platforms themselves say drives ranking. The documented picture is simpler than most "algorithm" articles suggest.

Apple Podcasts search orders results by metadata (show name, channel name, episode titles), popularity (followers and plays in Apple Podcasts), and engagement from search results (plays and follows that happen after a search). Apple states that ratings, reviews, and shares are not factored directly into search results, while noting they "influence whether people will listen or follow, and those factors influence the charts." Source: How Search works on Apple Podcasts.

Apple Podcasts charts reflect a mix of listening, follows, and completion rate. Source: Apple Podcasts Charts.

Spotify emphasizes searchable titles and descriptions (the first 250 characters matter most), transcripts, chapters, and topic/guest tags, and notes that click-through from search influences relevance. It does not publish ranking weights. Source: Words matter: podcast SEO on Spotify.

Everything beyond that — precise completion-rate thresholds, subscriber-conversion percentages that "trigger" a boost, how many episodes per session an algorithm wants — is undisclosed. Treat any such figure, including the illustrative examples below, as a hypothesis to test against your own data.

Why Downloads Alone Mislead ​

Downloads count a file being requested. They don't tell you whether the listener found you through search, how much they listened, whether they followed, or whether they came back. Different measurement services also count differently (see why running multiple trackers causes problems), so even the raw number is less absolute than it looks.

None of that makes downloads useless. They are the right denominator for many ratios. They are simply the wrong metric to watch on its own if your question is "am I becoming easier to find?"

Metric 1: Keyword Positions (PodSEO) ​

The most direct SEO metric is where you rank for the searches you care about, on each platform, over time. This is what PodSEO observes for you.

  • Keyword Tracker records your position for each tracked keyword on each platform and shows movement over time.
  • Show Rankings and Episode Rankings compare positions across platforms for the same term.
  • Show Visibility and Episode Visibility surface the keywords you already rank for without tracking them first.

How to read positions: a position is a finding signal, not an attribution signal. Ranking #3 for "beginner investing podcast" means listeners who search that phrase can see you near the top. It does not tell you how many of them tapped play. Read positions alongside listening data rather than converting them into estimated listeners.

Metric 2: Engagement (Apple Podcasts Connect) ​

Apple's analytics report followers, listeners, engaged listeners (people who listened to at least 20 minutes or 40% of an episode), plays, time listened, top countries and cities, and episode comparisons by days since release. Source: Apple listener analytics.

Apple's analytics do not report how a listener found your show. There is no search-vs-browse acquisition breakdown, so don't build a measurement plan around one. What Apple does give you lines up neatly with what Apple says its search and charts consider: plays, follows, and how much of an episode people consume.

Useful views:

  • Followers over time — follows are one of Apple's named popularity and chart inputs.
  • Engaged listeners ÷ listeners — Apple's own definition of meaningful consumption. Track the ratio per episode and compare episodes to each other, not to an external benchmark.
  • Performance by days since release — lets you compare a new episode's first week against your own previous episodes at the same age.

Metric 3: Discovery Sources (Spotify for Creators) ​

Spotify's Discovery analytics report impressions — how many times your show's content was shown to a user on a discovery surface — split by source: Home (recommendations, recently played shows, previews), Library (saved shows and playlists), and Search. It also reports conversion rate, plays, and average completion, which is where exposure turns into listening. Read the definition of the Search source carefully — Spotify says it covers "search queries … top charts, and our editorial recommendations." Source: Spotify discovery analytics.

So Spotify's Search source is best understood as "people who found you rather than being pushed you," mixing keyword search with charts and editorial — and it counts impressions, not listeners. It's still worth tracking: if Search impressions grow in the same period your Spotify positions improve in Keyword Tracker, and conversion rate holds, that's a reasonable sign your metadata changes are working. Just don't present it as pure keyword-search traffic or as acquired listeners.

Metric 4: Ratings and Reviews (Apple, Spotify) ​

Apple says ratings, reviews, and shares are not a direct search factor. They are still worth tracking, for two reasons.

First, they are a proxy for consumption. People generally review shows they finish and return to, so review growth tends to track the plays, follows, and completions that Apple does measure. Second, they influence whether a new listener who finds you in search decides to tap play — and engagement from search results is a named Apple factor.

PodSEO's Ratings and Reviews view tracks counts and averages per country so you can see review growth alongside ranking movement. Ask for reviews with a specific prompt tied to an episode rather than a generic request; it produces more useful feedback and reflects real listening.

Metric 5: Charts (PodSEO, Apple, Spotify) ​

Chart positions are a separate system from search and depend on listening, follows, and completion in a given country and category. My Charts tracks your chart positions over time; All Charts lets you see who is charting in your category. Chart movement is a useful lagging indicator of popularity — the mechanism Apple says also feeds search.

Metrics to Retire (or Relabel) ​

A few metrics show up in podcast SEO advice with a precision the evidence doesn't support.

  • "Completion rate above X% guarantees ranking improvement." Completion is a documented Apple chart input, but no threshold is published. Track your own completion trend per episode and compare against your own median.
  • "Healthy shows convert Y% of listeners to subscribers." No platform publishes a conversion benchmark. Track followers gained per episode over time and watch the trend, not a target.
  • "Search traffic from Google Podcasts." Google Podcasts was discontinued in 2024 and replaced by podcasts in YouTube Music. YouTube Studio reports views, watch time, and traffic sources for podcast videos; treat it as a video platform with its own analytics, not a substitute for the old Google Podcasts data. Source: YouTube's announcement.
  • "Review velocity is weighted by the algorithm." See above: reviews are an indirect signal. Track them, but don't expect a review campaign alone to move a search position.

If you do keep a benchmark from a third-party study, note its source, date, sample, and definition next to the number. If it's just an example, label it as one.

A Review Routine That Works ​

Because the platforms don't disclose weights, the most reliable approach is a consistent cadence and a baseline of your own.

Weekly (10 minutes): open Keyword Tracker and note any keyword that moved more than a few positions on any platform. Check whether a recent title, description, or transcript change lines up with the movement.

Per episode (first two weeks): in Apple's analytics, compare the new episode's followers gained and engaged-listener ratio against your previous three episodes at the same days-since-release. In Spotify, note impressions by Home / Library / Search source and the conversion rate.

Monthly (30 minutes): review Show Visibility for newly ranked keywords you aren't tracking yet, check chart movement in My Charts, and look at review growth in Ratings and Reviews. Record three numbers — tracked keywords in the top 10, followers gained, engaged-listener ratio — so next month has a baseline.

Quarterly: revisit your Primary keywords in Keyword Strategy. If a term hasn't moved in a quarter despite title and description changes, it may be too competitive for now; the Keyword Coverage view helps find adjacent terms with less competition.

Optimization results don't arrive on a fixed schedule. Some title changes show up in positions within days; popularity-driven movement builds over months. Give a change at least a few weeks and one or two new episodes before judging it, and compare against your own prior numbers rather than a promised timeline.

Competitive Benchmarking: Relative Performance ​

Your numbers exist in context. Competitive Analysis shows which keywords competitors rank for and where you overlap, which is a far better yardstick than a universal completion-rate figure. A niche interview show and a daily news show will have very different engagement profiles; comparing yourself to shows with a similar format and audience is the only comparison that means anything.

Put this into practice ​

Pair your review with the platform search guide and the 30-day action plan. Use Keyword Tracker to follow selected search terms; keep using Apple, Spotify, and your host's analytics for listening and download data.
