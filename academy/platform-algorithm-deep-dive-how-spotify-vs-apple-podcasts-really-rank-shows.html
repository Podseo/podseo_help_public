Platform Algorithm Deep Dive: How Spotify vs Apple Podcasts Really Rank Shows ​

If you've ever wondered why your podcast ranks #3 on Apple Podcasts but doesn't crack the top 50 on Spotify, you're not alone. Each platform runs its own search engine with its own ranking logic, and neither publishes the full recipe. What they do publish is more useful than most "algorithm secrets" articles admit, and it's a much safer foundation for your strategy.

This guide separates four things that are often blended together: search results, charts, editorial features, and personalized recommendations. Each has different inputs, and advice that works for one can be irrelevant for another.

What We Know vs. What We Guess ​

Before going platform by platform, a quick note on evidence. Apple and Spotify both publish creator guidance describing what their search considers. Neither publishes weights, refresh cycles, or thresholds. Any article that tells you a specific percentage, a "48–72 hour indexing window," or a best day of the week to publish is describing a guess, not a documented rule.

Throughout this article, statements marked as documented come from official platform guidance (linked). Everything else is labeled as a working assumption you should test against your own data in Keyword Tracker.

Apple Podcasts: Search ​

Apple's own explanation of Search on Apple Podcasts is short and worth reading in full: How Search works on Apple Podcasts.

Documented search factors (Apple):

  • Metadata — the show name, channel name, and episode titles. Apple advises making these "specific and unique" and avoiding generic names, names too similar to existing shows, emojis, and repeated episode titles.
  • Popularity — shows with large numbers of followers and plays in Apple Podcasts.
  • Engagement from search — shows that get played or followed from search results for a query rank higher for related terms over time.

What Apple says is not a direct search factor: ratings, reviews, and shares. Apple's wording is that they "help indicate a podcast's newness, popularity, and quality" but "are not factored into Search results." The same statement appears in Apple's charts documentation, with an important addition: they "influence whether people will listen or follow, and those factors influence the charts."

Read that carefully, because both halves matter. Ratings and reviews are not a switch Apple flips in its search index, but they are a strong proxy for consumption. A show that collects reviews is a show people are finishing and coming back to, and that behavior (plays, follows, completions) is exactly what Apple says it measures. Treat reviews as a signal you want to grow because it reflects — and helps drive — the popularity mechanism that clearly sits underneath Apple's ranking, not as a ranking factor you can optimize in isolation.

Working assumptions (not documented): description text and transcripts probably help Apple understand what a show is about, but Apple's search documentation only names show name, channel name, and episode titles as metadata inputs. Put your most important keyword in the title, not just the description.

PodSEO observation: the Author field punches above its weight. Across the rankings PodSEO collects, shows whose Author (the itunes:author field your host publishes) matches a query tend to rank far better on Apple than the documented inputs alone would predict — in our observations, an effect comparable to the title's. This is a pattern in PodSEO's data, not a controlled study, and Apple publishes no weights. The most likely explanation is where Apple's podcast search comes from: it grew out of the iTunes / Apple Music search engine, where people search for artists and bands and expect tracks and albums back. In podcast terms, the author is the "artist," the show is the "album," and episodes are the "tracks," so a query that matches the author field is treated as a strong match for the whole catalog. Apple doesn't document this, so treat it as an observed pattern rather than a rule — but it's why the Planner checks author alignment alongside the title, and why a descriptive author string ("Jane Doe — Personal Finance Coach" rather than a bare studio name) tends to help. Don't stuff it: keep it an honest name plus, at most, a short topical qualifier.

Apple Podcasts: Charts and Editorial ​

Apple's charts and its editorial features are separate systems from search, and they have separate inputs.

Charts (documented): Apple says Top Shows and Trending Episodes reflect a mix of listening, follows, and completion rate, refreshed throughout the day and computed per country. See Apple Podcasts Charts. PodSEO's My Charts pages track your positions across countries and categories.

Editorial (documented): Apple's editorial team selects features from submissions. Apple asks for at least two weeks of lead time and recommends requesting a Monday feature date, because that falls naturally within its planning cycle. See Optimize your request for promotion and PodSEO's PR Outreach guide.

Spotify: Search ​

Spotify published updated podcast SEO guidance on 26 August 2026: Words matter: podcast SEO on Spotify. It is more prescriptive than many creators expect, and it contradicts the old idea that Spotify "doesn't care about keywords."

Documented Spotify recommendations:

  • Show titles: keep them to 3–4 words, include a searchable keyword that reflects the content, and avoid generic words, special characters, and names that collide with existing shows.
  • Show descriptions: the first 250 characters are visible before the listener expands the description and are pulled into search snippets. Lead with your core topic, then work in secondary topics and guest types in full sentences.
  • Episode titles: mirror the way people actually type a search. Structure titles around search intent rather than intrigue.
  • Episode descriptions: 3–4 specific sentences naming guests, exact topics, and the questions the episode answers; don't repeat the title.
  • Transcripts, chapters, and tags: turn on automatic transcripts (Spotify indexes them at the keyword level), review chapter labels for accuracy, and verify guest and topic tags on eligible episodes.
  • Off-platform signals: shareable Spotify links, a podcast website with rich show notes, newsletters, and embedded players all help Spotify — and AI answer engines — understand and cite your show.

Undisclosed: Spotify does not publish ranking weights, and it does not say how much "binge behavior," session depth, or seasonality affect search. Those may well feed its personalized recommendations, but treat them as assumptions rather than levers you can measure.

Spotify: Home, Library, and Search Analytics ​

Spotify for Creators' Discovery analytics report impressions — the number of times your show's content was shown to a user on a discovery surface — broken down by where they came from: Home (recommendations, recently played, previews), Library (saved shows and playlists), and Search. Alongside impressions it reports conversion rate, plays, and average completion. Two cautions: an impression is exposure, not a listener (watch conversion rate and plays for that), and Spotify's definition of the Search source includes "search queries … top charts, and our editorial recommendations," so it is not a pure keyword-search number. See Spotify discovery analytics.

Practical Optimization That Works on Both Platforms ​

The good news is that the documented guidance from both platforms mostly points in the same direction.

Titles are the one metadata input both platforms name — and on Apple, Author appears to matter too. Apple lists show and episode titles among its search inputs (without saying how they're weighted); Spotify tells you to put a searchable keyword in the title. On Apple, PodSEO's ranking observations suggest the Author field carries more influence than most creators assume (see above; treat it as a hypothesis to test on your own keywords). Use Keyword Strategy to choose Primary keywords and the Planner to check title and author alignment before you publish.

Front-load descriptions. Spotify shows the first 250 characters before expansion. Put the topic, guest, and main question in that opening rather than a teaser.

Review transcripts and chapters. Spotify indexes transcripts and chapter labels. Add a five-minute check to your publishing workflow: confirm the automatic transcript spells your key terms, guest names, and product names correctly, and that chapter titles describe the actual segment.

Grow the behaviors that popularity measures. On Apple, plays and follows from search results feed back into ranking. On both platforms, follows, completions, and returning listeners are what "popularity" means in practice. Reviews and ratings are worth asking for because they reflect and reinforce that consumption, even though Apple says they're not a direct input.

Keep one title per episode. Some articles suggest different titles per platform. Most hosts publish a single RSS feed to every directory, so in practice you optimize one title, and the documented guidance above means a clear, specific, keyword-led title works on both.

Measuring Platform-Specific Success ​

Each tool exposes different data. Use each one for what it actually reports.

PodSEO gives you observed keyword positions on each platform. Show Rankings and Episode Rankings compare where you rank for the same term on Apple and Spotify; Keyword Tracker shows how those positions move over time.

Apple Podcasts Connect Analytics reports followers, listeners, engaged listeners, plays, time listened, top countries and cities, and episode performance comparisons. It does not report how a listener found your show (search vs. browse). See Apple listener analytics.

Spotify for Creators reports impressions by Home / Library / Search source as described above, plus conversion rate, plays, and completion. Remember that impressions are exposure, not plays, and that the Search source includes charts and editorial.

What not to do: don't treat a ranking position or an impression as an attributable listener. A #3 ranking tells you people can find you for that term; it doesn't tell you how many did. Read positions and listening data side by side, and compare against your own baseline rather than a universal benchmark.

The Algorithm Evolution Factor ​

Both platforms are moving toward richer content understanding. Spotify now indexes transcripts and chapters and explicitly talks about AI answer engines citing show metadata. Apple emphasizes engagement from search results. Neither direction rewards keyword stuffing; both reward specific, accurate metadata and content people finish.

The safest long-term bet is to write titles and descriptions that read like the search a listener would type, keep transcripts clean, and track the outcome in PodSEO instead of chasing undocumented weights.
