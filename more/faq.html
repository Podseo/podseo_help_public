FAQ ​

What are the pricing plans for PodSEO? ​

PodSEO offers multiple plans to fit different needs — from independent podcasters to agencies managing multiple shows. Each plan includes different features, such as keyword tracking limits, access to visibility insights, and competitor discovery tools.

You can find the full breakdown of our pricing and features on our Pricing Page.


Is there a free version of PodSEO? ​

Yes! PodSEO offers a free version with specific limitations — perfect if you're just getting started. Here's what you get:

Free Plan Includes:

  • Keyword Strategy: Up to 5 tracked keywords in total, which you can use for your show, your episodes, your brand, competitors or themes
  • Show Visibility: In Organic Visibility, you see every keyword row with its rankings and search volume; your 3 best-ranked keywords are shown in clear and the others are masked (upgrade to see them all)
  • Show optimization: Review how your show title, author, and description align with your show keywords
  • Planner: Plan up to 3 active episodes and assign each episode its own keywords; AI episode suggestions require a paid plan
  • Analytics: Connect listening sources on every plan; YouTube offers a limited Free view
  • Competitors: Track up to 2 competitors
  • Credits: 10 monthly credits for actions such as episode analysis and clip creation
  • LUCIA AI Chat: 5 messages per month
  • Keyword Tracker: Full access to tracking charts and historical data
  • Basic Platform Data: Rankings across Apple Podcasts, Spotify, Amazon Music, and YouTube Music

Paid Plan Benefits:

  • Full Visibility: Unlock all keyword rankings in Show Visibility
  • A much larger keyword allowance: Podcaster 100, Producer 500, Agency 50,000 tracked keywords in total
  • Complete Metadata Analysis: AI suggestions for show metadata in Show optimization, and episode suggestions in Planner
  • Advanced Features: Enhanced competitor discovery and premium analytics

When you're ready to unlock the full platform, you can upgrade at any time. Learn more on our Pricing Page.


What are show, episode, brand, competitor and theme keywords? ​

Every tracked keyword says what it's for. A keyword can serve the show and several episodes at once.

Show Keywords (start with 2-3, aim for about 5, with 2 in your show title):

  • Your most important target keywords for your show title, author and description
  • Generate the deepest analysis and recommendations
  • Should represent your core podcast topics

Episode Keywords (about 2-3 per episode, on every plan):

  • Guide the title and description of one planned or published episode
  • Provide broader topic coverage and discovery opportunities

Brand, Guest, Competitor and Theme Keywords:

  • Your show or host name, a guest's name, a competitor's terms, or a group of keywords
  • Tracked and analyzed, never scored against your metadata
  • Perfect for competitive intelligence and market monitoring

A keyword you haven't assigned yet stays Unassigned: it is still tracked, and PodSEO suggests an episode when the keyword already appears in one.

How the allowance works: each plan has one total keyword allowance shared across every podcast in your account (Free 5, Podcaster 100, Producer 500, Agency 50,000). Every tracked keyword draws from that pool, whatever it's used for; there is no separate cap per kind. Adding a second podcast does not add a second allowance.

Learn more about building your keyword strategy in our Keyword Strategy guide or follow our step-by-step PodSEO Workflow.


How often should I check my metadata alignment? ​

Review show metadata in Command Center → Show optimization and individual episodes in Command Center → Planner:

  • Initially: After adding your podcast and setting up your keyword strategy
  • After Changes: Whenever you update your cover art, description, or add new episodes
  • Monthly: As a regular optimization check to discover new opportunities
  • Before Big Launches: Prior to major episode releases or series launches

For show-wide suggestions on a paid plan, click Get AI suggestions for your podcast in Show optimization. In Planner, open the episode you want to improve and review its own keywords and suggestions.


Why do I see different rankings on different platforms? ​

Each podcasting platform runs its own search, and none of them publishes its full ranking formula. What they do say:

Apple Podcasts: Search is ordered by metadata (show name, channel name, episode titles), popularity (followers and plays), and engagement from search results. Ratings and reviews aren't a direct search input, but they reflect the listening and follows that are. In PodSEO's observations the Author field also appears to matter more than most creators expect on Apple. Spotify: Recommends searchable titles and descriptions (first 250 characters), transcripts, chapters, and topic/guest tags; click-through from search influences relevance. Amazon Music and YouTube Music: Neither publishes search guidance for podcasts; YouTube Music behaves like a video platform with its own search and recommendations.

This is why PodSEO tracks all major platforms — what works on one platform may need adjustment for others. Use Show Visibility to see your performance across all platforms, Competitive Analysis to see how competitors rank, and the platform algorithm guide for what's documented versus assumed.


Why are my rankings different from what I see in my own podcast app? ​

PodSEO collects rankings as a neutral listener in the United States sees them. Your own app shows results for your device's country, language, and sometimes your listening history, so small differences are normal. If your audience is mainly outside the US, paid plans let you add search countries per podcast in Settings → Shows and switch between them in Keyword Tracker (Podcaster 1 additional country, Producer 5, Agency 10). Search countries are separate from chart countries and review storefronts.


What does keyword search volume mean? ​

Search volume tells you roughly how many times a month people search for a keyword. PodSEO shows it as one of six levels, drawn as a 6-step green meter, each with a round monthly range:

LevelSearches per month
Very nicheUnder 100
Niche100–250
Small250–1K
Medium1K–2.5K
Popular2.5K–10K
Very popular10K+

In narrow table columns you see the meter only: hover over it (or focus it with the keyboard) to read the level and range, for example "Small · 250–1K searches/mo". Wider spots, such as the keyword search dropdown in Keyword Strategy and the Keyword Tracker cards on mobile, show the range next to the meter. A clock icon means the keyword hasn't been measured yet; its level fills in after the next periodic check.

Volume isn't good or bad on its own. A popular keyword brings more searches but usually more shows competing for them, while a niche keyword brings fewer searches that are often easier to win and come from listeners who know exactly what they want. Use volume together with Difficulty and Popularity (how crowded the keyword is) to pick the right mix for your show.


How long does it take to see results from optimization? ​

There is no fixed timeline, because platforms don't publish how quickly they re-index metadata or how heavily they weight popularity signals. What you can expect:

Soon after a change:

  • Your updated title or description appears on the platforms once your host's feed refreshes and each platform re-fetches it (usually hours to a few days)
  • New keywords begin tracking in PodSEO with the next ranking collection

Over the following weeks:

  • Metadata-driven position changes for the keywords you targeted often show up within a few weeks; watch them in Keyword Tracker
  • Popularity-driven movement (followers, plays, completions) builds more slowly and depends on how many new listeners your episodes attract

Rather than waiting for a promised milestone, compare each keyword against its own starting position, give a change at least a few weeks and a new episode or two before judging it, and keep applying recommendations consistently. The analytics guide explains a review routine.


What happens after I add a podcast? ​

When you add a podcast, PodSEO sets up your baseline across Apple Podcasts, Spotify, Amazon Music, and YouTube Music — capturing your current rankings, the keywords you already appear for, and similar podcasts in your category. This gives you a starting point to measure your optimization efforts against.

Your dashboard will populate as this information becomes available, and you'll be notified when everything is ready to explore.


Can I track keywords I'm not currently ranking for? ​

Yes! Track any keyword and leave it unassigned (or assign it to a competitor or theme), whether you're currently ranking or not. This is perfect for:

  • Competitor Analysis: Track keywords your competitors rank for
  • Market Research: Monitor emerging trends in your niche
  • Future Strategy: Watch keywords you plan to target later
  • Performance Goals: Track progress toward ranking for new terms

These keywords count toward your plan's total keyword allowance.

Unassigned terms can contribute to your show-description alignment and suggestions. An episode without its own keywords uses your show keywords, then Unassigned terms if no show keywords are set.

Assigning a term only to a competitor or theme does not make it a metadata target; an additional Show or Episode target still does. Learn more in our Keyword Strategy guide or explore competitor discovery tools to find new keywords to track.

Can PodSEO show how my podcast performs on YouTube? ​

Yes. Connect your podcast's YouTube channel and activate YouTube Insights (Analytics → YouTube). You'll see your channel's views, watch hours, subscriber change, where your views come from, and the real searches that bring viewers to your videos, compared with the keywords you track in PodSEO. PodSEO also matches your videos to your podcast episodes and suggests tracked keywords to add to your video titles.

The connection is read-only: PodSEO never edits your videos. It's the same connection used to publish Shorts from Social Publishing. YouTube Insights is available on every plan; the free plan covers channel performance, one search term, and your latest episode, while paid plans unlock every search term and your recent episodes.

YouTube vs YouTube Music: YouTube Music is the podcast directory inside the YouTube Music app, and PodSEO tracks your podcast rankings there like on Apple and Spotify. YouTube Insights is about your YouTube channel and its videos.
