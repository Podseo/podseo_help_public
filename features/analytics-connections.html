Connect Your Analytics ​

All Your Numbers in One Place, Next to Your Rankings ​

Your listening numbers are scattered: Spotify for Creators has one set, Apple Podcasts Connect another, YouTube Studio a third, and your podcast host yet another. Analytics connections bring them into PodSEO, so you can see plays, listeners, followers and downloads on the same screen as your keyword rankings and visibility, and judge whether your SEO work is turning into an audience.

Every connection is free on every plan, including the Free plan. You can connect as many sources as apply to your show; use the source bar to switch between the reports available for your show.

To access: Click Analytics in the sidebar. Select Spotify, YouTube, Apple Podcasts, your host (Spreaker, Buzzsprout, Simplecast, Captivate, Transistor or Podigee), Tracker (OP3), or hopr.fm, as applicable. The Overview source tab compares your connected reports; see Reading Analytics.

To manage existing access: Open Settings → Connections, filter by podcast, and expand the provider. You can also follow Manage connections links from the product.

Who can connect: In a personal account, you can connect and disconnect everything. In an organization, only Admins and Owners can connect or disconnect a source; other team members can view the numbers.

Read-only analytics: Analytics reads reports rather than changing your podcast or videos. Use the connection method shown for each source. Buzzsprout and Simplecast use an account API token (Simplecast's is read-only); the other supported private sources use invitations or their own sign-in screens.

For the connected screens, see the dedicated report guides: Spotify, Apple Podcasts, Hosting, OP3 and hopr.fm. This page focuses on establishing and managing access.

What happens after I connect? ​

A connection being accepted and its reports being ready are separate steps. Read the status message: it tells you whether PodSEO is waiting for the invitation, needs your confirmation, or is importing reports. Connected does not mean the provider has released figures for today's episode.

Spotify for Creators ​

Spotify's own data on how listeners discover and play your show: where impressions come from, how many turn into plays, and who your listeners are.

How to connect ​

Your show needs to be linked to Spotify in PodSEO first (you can check this on the Distribution Health page).

  1. Open Analytics and select the Spotify tab. Click Connect Spotify to get your show's code. The code is valid for 30 days.
  2. In Spotify Manage, open Teams, choose the podcast's team, and invite spotify@podseo.com.
  3. Paste the PodSEO code into the invite's Title field. This is how PodSEO knows the invite comes from you. Leave Set team member as team admin off, then click Next.
  4. Set Analytics to Reader and leave everything else (Episodes, Interact, Monetize, Settings) on No access.
  5. Send the invite, then return to PodSEO and click I've sent the invite. Follow the status card while PodSEO confirms access and imports your reports.

If PodSEO is already on your Spotify team but cannot read analytics, edit that member's permissions in Spotify Manage: set Analytics → Reader. You do not need to send another invite. Return to PodSEO and use Check access now when offered. If PodSEO was removed from the team, follow Connect again to send a new invitation.

Someone else manages the show's team on Spotify (a producer, network or agency)? Forward them the steps and your code: the connection still comes to your PodSEO account.

Can't find your podcast in Spotify Manage? ​

If your podcast isn't in the list of teams, or Spotify says "You're not on any teams yet", one of these is usually the reason.

Your hosting platform put the show on Spotify for you, or you've never used Spotify for Creators. Many hosts send your show to Spotify automatically, so it exists on Spotify but nobody has claimed it yet. Claiming is free:

  1. Sign in at Spotify for Creators with any Spotify account. Already manage another show? Open your profile menu and choose Add a new show.
  2. Choose Find an existing show, then Somewhere else, and enter your RSS feed or your Spotify show link.
  3. Spotify emails an 8-digit code to the email address in your RSS feed. Enter it to finish the claim.
  4. Your show now has a team in Spotify Manage: follow the connection steps above.

Some hosts leave your email out of the RSS feed by default. If the code never arrives, add your email to the feed in your host's settings, then try again. Your host's own guide to claiming on Spotify usually shows where. In PodSEO, after you start the connection, open Problems connecting? on the Spotify tab: it shows your host's guide and checks whether your feed has an email Spotify can use.

Someone else already claimed the show, or you have several logins. A show can be claimed only once. Try Spotify Manage with your other logins (personal, work, Google or Apple sign-in), or ask the current owner or admin to add you to the team. They can also send the PodSEO invite for you. Don't create a duplicate show.

Spotify's own guide: Claiming your podcast on Spotify for Creators.

Wondering whether claiming is worth it when your host already sends the show to Spotify? See Why You Should Claim Your Podcast on Apple Podcasts and Spotify.

What you'll see ​

  • Daily plays over time
  • Discovery: impressions from Search, Home, Library and Other, and the funnel from impression to play, so you can see how often being seen turns into being listened to
  • Engagement: new followers, hours listened (compared with the previous 30 days), and new versus returning listeners
  • Audience: age, gender, countries, regions, cities, devices and listening apps
  • Episodes: each episode's impressions, impressions from Search, and plays

Spotify's Search source includes search results as well as charts and editorial exposure. Use it alongside your rankings, rather than treating every Search impression as a keyword search.

How fresh ​

The plays overview is updated daily. Audience, engagement, discovery, benchmarks and episode reports can refresh on different schedules and retain their own reporting windows. Read the dates on each report; Spotify analytics explains the individual views.

Disconnecting ​

Open Settings → Connections, expand Spotify for the correct show, and choose Disconnect…. Confirm after reviewing the notice. PodSEO stops reading your Spotify analytics and your Spotify numbers no longer show anywhere in PodSEO; PodSEO keeps them for when you reconnect. To end PodSEO's access completely, also remove spotify@podseo.com from your show's team in Spotify for Creators.

Apple Podcasts Connect ​

Apple's data on plays, listeners, followers and how much of each episode people actually hear.

How to connect ​

Your show must be listed on Apple Podcasts.

  1. Open Analytics and select the Apple Podcasts tab.
  2. In Apple Podcasts Connect, go to Account > People and invite applepodcasts@podseo.com.
  3. Give the invite the Analyst role and select your show.
  4. Send the invitation. PodSEO checks for new invitations every 10 minutes.

Only the show's owner or one of its Admins in Apple Podcasts Connect can send the invite. If someone else manages the show on Apple (a producer, network or agency), forward them these steps.

Can't find your show in Apple Podcasts Connect? ​

Your hosting platform put the show on Apple for you, or you never had an Apple Podcasts Connect account. Many hosts submit shows to Apple under their own Apple account, so the show isn't in yours and there's nobody to invite from. Apple lets you claim it, free:

  1. Copy your show's Apple Podcasts link (the Apple Podcasts tab in PodSEO shows it).
  2. Sign in to Apple Podcasts Connect with your own Apple Account (create a Podcasts Connect account if you need one). At the bottom of the page, under More Actions, click Claim Existing Show, paste the link and generate a token. Check the show's name and artwork before you do.
  3. Paste the token in your hosting platform's Apple claim field. Every host names it differently; in PodSEO, after you start the connection, open Problems connecting? on the Apple Podcasts tab to see where your host keeps it, a link to your host's own guide, and whether the token is in your feed yet.
  4. Apple usually moves the show to your account within 24 hours; some hosts advise allowing a few days. Once it appears, follow the connection steps above.

The token is valid for 7 days. Some shows can't be claimed this way: claiming disabled for the show, shows in a channel or with a subscription, Apple-hosted audio, no RSS feed, or a show not available on Apple Podcasts. If your host has no claim field, ask its support. Apple's own guide: Claim your show.

Someone else manages the show, or you have several Apple logins. Ask the show's owner or an Admin to send the invite, or try your other Apple accounts.

Why claim, if your host already puts the show on Apple? ​

Nothing changes at your host or for listeners: the show stays hosted where it is and Apple keeps reading the same RSS feed. Apple charges nothing to claim. With the show in your own Apple Podcasts Connect account you can:

  • See Apple's own listening data: followers, listeners, engaged listeners, plays, time listened, top countries and cities, per episode. Your host reports downloads, not this. Apple's guide: Listener analytics.
  • Upload artwork such as Full Page Show Art, a tall version of your cover shown on your Apple show page and in the player (Apple's template).
  • Check your show's status on Apple and Refresh Feed when a new episode hasn't appeared yet (Refresh a podcast).
  • Add people with their own roles, such as PodSEO's view-only Analyst invite, which changes nothing in your show.

The longer version, for Spotify too: Why You Should Claim Your Podcast on Apple Podcasts and Spotify.

Proving the show is yours ​

Before showing Apple data, PodSEO checks that the show belongs to you:

  • If the owner email in your RSS feed matches a verified email on your PodSEO account (or on your organization's account), the check is automatic.
  • Otherwise, PodSEO sends a one-time link to the owner email in your RSS feed. The link is valid for 14 days. Whoever reads that mailbox clicks Yes, this is us to confirm, or No, I don't know PodSEO to refuse.
  • If your RSS feed has no owner email, PodSEO checks whether your show's website (from the feed) and a verified email on your PodSEO account share the same domain. If they don't, the PodSEO team confirms the show with you.

Once one show is proven, other shows from the same Apple Podcasts Connect team are approved too.

What you'll see ​

  • Plays and Hours listened for the selected period, with a daily plays chart; Listeners retains Apple's own last-60-days window
  • Followers: total, gained, lost and net
  • Time listened by followers compared with everyone else
  • Monthly history of Plays, Listeners, Engaged listeners and Hours
  • Episodes table with Plays, Listeners, Engaged, Avg. listened, First 7 days and First 30 days for each episode, plus a retention curve per episode showing where listeners drop off
  • Audience: countries, top cities and the available follower comparisons, each with its own reporting window

Use the 30, 90 and 365 days controls for the reports that follow the selected period. Listener totals, episode measurements and audience snapshots retain their own labelled windows.

How fresh ​

Apple data is read daily. Apple provides a limited daily history; PodSEO retains the days already read, so longer views build up over time. Each report keeps its own dates. See Apple Podcasts analytics for coverage, episode reports and unavailable values.

Disconnecting ​

Open Settings → Connections, expand Apple Podcasts for the correct show, and choose Disconnect…. PodSEO stops reading your Apple analytics and your Apple numbers no longer show anywhere in PodSEO; PodSEO keeps them for when you reconnect. To end PodSEO's access completely, also remove applepodcasts@podseo.com from Account > People in Apple Podcasts Connect.

YouTube ​

How people find and watch your podcast on YouTube, including the real search terms that bring viewers in.

How to connect ​

  1. Open Analytics and select the YouTube tab (or click it to open the YouTube Insights page).
  2. Click Connect YouTube and sign in with the Google account (or Brand Account) that owns your podcast's channel.
  3. You return to Analytics → YouTube. Check the channel's name. Your first connection activates Insights automatically and starts importing the last six months. If the page shows Activate Insights for an existing connection, click it; no new sign-in is needed.

What you'll see ​

  • Views, Watch hours, Subscribers (net) and the % of views from YouTube search, with a daily views chart
  • Traffic sources and external referrers
  • Search terms YouTube reports, compared with the keywords you track in PodSEO
  • Episode-to-video matching, with keyword-based title suggestions for your videos
  • Audience: countries, age and gender, cities, US states, operating systems and playback locations
  • Your podcast playlists

Use the 7, 28 and 90 days tabs to change the period.

How fresh ​

Core figures are updated daily; the breakdowns (such as audience and traffic details) weekly.

Free plan limit ​

On the Free plan you see channel performance, one reported search term and your latest episode. Paid plans show everything.

Disconnecting ​

Disconnecting YouTube removes its analytics data unless that channel is still connected to another of your podcasts. A broken authorization has a 30-day retention window before deletion. Read the confirmation in Settings → Connections before proceeding. For the full walkthrough of YouTube, see YouTube Insights.

Hosting (Spreaker, Buzzsprout, Simplecast, Captivate, Transistor and Podigee) ​

Downloads straight from your podcast host. Spreaker, Buzzsprout, Simplecast, Captivate, Transistor and Podigee are supported.

How to connect ​

The host tab (labelled Spreaker, Buzzsprout, Simplecast, Captivate, Transistor or Podigee) appears when your podcast's feed is hosted on one of them.

Spreaker

  1. Open Analytics and select your Spreaker tab.
  2. Click Sign in to Spreaker.
  3. Approve the request with Authorize. You're sent back to PodSEO and the first sync begins.

Buzzsprout

  1. In Buzzsprout, open your profile and its API section, then copy the API token of the account that hosts your podcast.
  2. In PodSEO, open Analytics and select your Buzzsprout tab.
  3. Paste the token and click Connect Buzzsprout. PodSEO matches your podcast through its RSS feed and the first sync begins.

A Buzzsprout token can also edit your Buzzsprout account. PodSEO stores it encrypted and only uses it to read reports, never to change anything.

Simplecast

  1. In Simplecast, open the account menu (top right) → Account Settings → Private Apps, then copy the Read-Only API Token.
  2. In PodSEO, open Analytics and select your Simplecast tab.
  3. Paste the token and click Connect Simplecast. PodSEO matches your podcast through its RSS feed and the first sync begins.

The Simplecast token is read-only: it can't change anything in your Simplecast account. PodSEO stores it encrypted.

Captivate

  1. In Captivate, click your name (top right) → My Account → API key, then click Reveal API Key.
  2. Copy both the User ID and the API Key.
  3. In PodSEO, open Analytics and select your Captivate tab.
  4. Paste both values and click Connect Captivate. PodSEO matches your podcast through its RSS feed (or, for a feed on your own domain, its newest episodes) and the first sync begins.

A Captivate API key can also edit your Captivate shows. PodSEO stores it encrypted and only uses it to read reports, never to change anything.

Transistor

  1. In Transistor, click your avatar (top right) → Manage Your Account.
  2. In the API Access section, copy Your API Key.
  3. In PodSEO, open Analytics and select your Transistor tab.
  4. Paste the key and click Connect Transistor. PodSEO matches your podcast through its RSS feed (or, for a feed behind a custom domain or a tracking prefix, its newest episodes) and the first sync begins.

A Transistor API key can also edit your Transistor shows. PodSEO stores it encrypted and only uses it to read reports, never to change anything.

Podigee

  1. In Podigee, open the account menu (your name, bottom left) → Data & integrations.
  2. Under API keys, click Create MCP token. Name it PodSEO, choose Read only and Until I remove it, then click Create token.
  3. Copy the token: Podigee shows it only once. It starts with pdg_pat_.
  4. In PodSEO, open Analytics, select your Podigee tab, paste the token and click Connect Podigee. PodSEO matches your podcast through its RSS feed (or, for a feed on your own domain, its newest episodes) and the first sync begins.

This works on every Podigee plan, Launch included. Your show needs at least one published episode: Podigee shares no statistics before that. A Read only token cannot change anything in your Podigee account.

What you'll see ​

  • Downloads, Daily average and Best day for the period, with a daily chart
  • Episodes with Total downloads, First 7 days and First 30 days, so you can compare launches fairly
  • Unique listeners per week, as Spreaker or Simplecast report them (Simplecast only when your Simplecast plan includes them; Buzzsprout doesn't report unique listeners)
  • Who listens: countries, apps, devices and operating systems, when your Spreaker plan includes them. With Buzzsprout: countries, devices and the apps listeners download each episode with, day by day. With Simplecast: countries, apps, devices and operating systems. With Captivate: countries, apps, devices and operating systems, for the show and for each episode (Captivate's weekly unique listeners aren't shown). Transistor reports downloads only: you get the downloads overview and the episode comparison, without listening apps, countries or unique listeners. Podigee reports downloads and the apps listeners use, for the show and for each episode, without countries, devices or unique listeners

How fresh ​

Downloads are synced daily. Spreaker audience details refresh weekly and the first sync backfills the last 365 days. Buzzsprout audience details refresh daily and the first sync reads all the history Buzzsprout still has. Simplecast audience details refresh weekly and the first sync reads your show's whole history. Captivate audience details refresh weekly and the first sync reads everything since your show joined Captivate. Transistor's first sync reads everything since your show was created on Transistor. Podigee's history goes back as far as your Podigee plan keeps statistics (7 days on Launch, 90 days on Starter, 6 months on Grow, 12 months on Scale, everything on Creator and above); PodSEO keeps every day it has read from then on, so connect early.

Disconnecting ​

Open Settings → Connections, expand your host for the correct show, and choose Disconnect…. PodSEO stops reading your downloads and deletes the access it stored. Your host numbers no longer show anywhere in PodSEO; PodSEO keeps them for when you reconnect.

  • Spreaker: PodSEO also asks Spreaker to revoke its access. You can check the connected apps in your Spreaker settings.
  • Buzzsprout: Buzzsprout has no way for PodSEO to revoke a token. To invalidate it, delete or regenerate it in your Buzzsprout profile.
  • Simplecast: to invalidate the token, regenerate it in Simplecast under Account Settings → Private Apps.
  • Captivate: to invalidate the key, click Regenerate API Key in Captivate under My Account → API key. Other apps that use the same key will need reconnecting.
  • Transistor: to invalidate the key, click Expire API Key in Transistor under Manage Your Account → API Access. Other apps that use the same key will need reconnecting.
  • Podigee: to invalidate the token, click Remove next to it in Podigee under Data & integrations → API keys.

OP3 (Tracker tab) ​

OP3 is a free, open podcast download tracker that many podcasters add to their feed as a prefix.

How to connect ​

There's nothing to connect. If your RSS feed already carries the OP3 prefix, PodSEO finds it on its own (it checks your feed weekly) and the Tracker tab shows your numbers.

What you'll see ​

  • Downloads per episode at 1, 7 and 30 days after release
  • Your Latest, Typical and Best episode, so you can tell at a glance whether a new episode is doing better or worse than usual
  • A separate total of downloads from listening apps, which excludes web browsers. The episode counts include browser downloads; see OP3 data.

How fresh ​

OP3 data is public, so anyone can look at it, and it covers about 60 days of history.

Disconnecting ​

Because PodSEO reads public OP3 data, there's no connection to remove. To stop OP3 tracking altogether, remove the OP3 prefix in your host's settings.

hopr.fm (PodSEO's Download Prefix) ​

hopr.fm is PodSEO's own download prefix. Like other analytics prefixes, it sits in front of your audio files and counts each download on its way to the listener. It's useful when your host doesn't give you good download numbers, or when you want a second, independent count.

How to connect ​

  1. Open Analytics and select the hopr.fm tab.
  2. Click Get my prefix. PodSEO creates your personal prefix in the form https://hopr.fm/k/<your key>/.
  3. In your podcast host, find the prefix or analytics prefix field (hosts name it differently) and paste the prefix there. It works alongside any other prefixes you already use.
  4. Save. Back in PodSEO you can ask it to re-check your feed, to confirm the prefix is in place.

What you'll see ​

  • Downloads for the period, with the change compared with the previous period
  • Apps, countries and episodes
  • Not counted: the requests that were left out and why: cloud apps, servers, bots, preloads, and requests with no user agent. A toggle lets you include cloud apps in the count if you want to.

Only your account can see your hopr.fm reports.

How downloads are counted ​

hopr.fm counts one download per day, per episode, per listener. To tell listeners apart without storing who they are, each listener is turned into a one-way code that changes every day. The method follows the IAB v2.3 measurement guidelines as far as a redirect prefix allows, but hopr.fm is not IAB certified.

Listener privacy ​

hopr.fm only redirects the request to your audio file. It sets no cookies, deletes raw logs and per-listener records after 35 days, never sells data or builds ad profiles, and podcasters only ever see totals. The full listener privacy policy is at podseo.com/hoprfm/privacy.

How fresh ​

Downloads are processed throughout the day.

Disconnecting ​

Remove the hopr.fm prefix from your host's settings. Downloads that no longer pass through the prefix aren't counted.

FAQ ​

Do I have to share my passwords with PodSEO? ​

No. Spotify and Apple Podcasts are connected by inviting a PodSEO address to your show's team with read-only analytics access. YouTube and Spreaker use their own sign-in screens, where you approve access directly with Google or Spreaker. Buzzsprout, Simplecast, Captivate, Transistor and Podigee use an API token or key you copy from your host account (Simplecast's and Podigee's are read-only), not your password. PodSEO never sees your passwords, and you can disconnect at any time.

Which podcast hosts can I connect? ​

Spreaker, Buzzsprout, Simplecast, Captivate, Transistor and Podigee. If your host isn't supported yet, you can still get download numbers from OP3 (if your feed uses it) or by adding the free hopr.fm prefix, and connect Spotify, Apple Podcasts and YouTube regardless of where you host.

What's the difference between hopr.fm and OP3? ​

Both are download prefixes that count downloads on the way to the listener. OP3 is an independent, open service: its data is public to anyone and PodSEO shows about 60 days of it, with downloads per episode at 1, 7 and 30 days. hopr.fm is PodSEO's own prefix: its reports are private to your account, show apps, countries, episodes and a breakdown of what wasn't counted, and you choose whether to count cloud apps. You can use both at the same time.

Is this really free? ​

Yes. Every connection is available on every plan, including Free. The one difference is YouTube: on the Free plan you see channel performance, one reported search term and your latest episode, while paid plans show every search term and all your recent episodes.

Why don't my numbers match exactly between sources? ​

Each platform measures something different: Spotify and Apple count plays and listeners in their own apps, YouTube counts views, and hosts and prefixes count downloads across all apps. That's why PodSEO shows each source on its own tab instead of mixing them into one number.

I'm a team member and can't connect. Why? ​

In an organization, only Admins and Owners can connect or disconnect analytics sources. Ask one of them to set up the connection; once it's done, everyone on the team can see the numbers.

Pro Tips ​

  • Start with the platforms where most of your audience listens, then add your host or a download prefix for a count across all apps.
  • Compare Spotify's impressions from Search with your Keyword Tracker rankings: rising rankings should show up as more search impressions.
  • Use the First 7 days and First 30 days columns (Apple and your host) to compare episodes fairly, regardless of how long ago they were published.
  • Check the Apple retention curve for episodes with a big drop early on: it shows where listeners leave, so you can compare it with how the episode opens.
  • If your host isn't supported yet, add the hopr.fm prefix: it takes a minute and gives you download numbers from that day on.
